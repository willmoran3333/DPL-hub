How this is built

Every roster is simulated through the real 38-week schedule {{ "{:,}".format(rk.sims) }} times. Each week a player is first drawn as available or not; the manager then picks the best legal eleven from whoever is fit — on expected points, never on what actually happens, so nobody gets hindsight — and those eleven score. Expected points blend this league's own 2025/26 per-90 rates, re-scored under the current rules, with the FPL price of each player, since price carries this-season information that last season's numbers cannot see. Teammates share a weekly club shock, so a stacked roster booms and blanks together.

Rosters are not frozen. Waivers and trades are modelled as drift: by the final week each team has closed {{ (100 * rk.params.reversion_by_season_end)|round|int }}% of its opening gap to the league average. Availability is regressed hard toward baseline (persistence {{ rk.params.avail_persist }}) — a player who missed half of last season is not permanently fragile, and taking last year's fitness at face value ranks managers on luck rather than squads.

Read the tiers, not the ordering. The leading group is stable across every plausible setting of the model's assumptions; positions in the middle of the table move around freely when those assumptions are nudged. Treat a two-point gap in the middle as noise, and note that the club-stacking penalty people expect turns out to be almost nothing — across eleven starters, one club's swing washes out.
